A **Galois extension** is an algebraic [[Field extension]] $E/F$ that is both [[Normal extension|normal]] and [[Separable extension|separable]]. Equivalently, the field fixed by the automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$ is exactly $F$. A Galois extension has a [[Galois group]], and obeys the [[Fundamental theorem of Galois theory]].

## Characterizations

For a finite extension $E/F$, a theorem of Emil Artin says that these are equivalent:

1. $E/F$ is Galois.
2. $E/F$ is normal and separable.
3. $E$ is a [[Splitting field]] of a set of [[Separable polynomial|separable polynomials]] over $F$.
4. $|\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)| = [E : F]$.
5. $F$ is the fixed field of a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(E)$.

## Examples

- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois, as the splitting field of $x^2 - 2$.
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is separable but not Galois. It lies in $\mathbb{R}$, where $x^3 - 2$ has one root, so its only automorphism is the identity.
- $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is Galois, and its group is generated by the [[Frobenius endomorphism]].
- An algebraic closure $\overline{K}/K$ is Galois if and only if $K$ is a [[Perfect field]].

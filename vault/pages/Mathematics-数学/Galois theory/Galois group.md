The **Galois group** of a [[Galois extension]] $E/F$ is the group, under composition, of the automorphisms of $E$ that fix every element of $F$:

$$\operatorname{Gal}(E/F) = \{\sigma : E \to E \mid \sigma(x) = x \text{ for all } x \in F\}.$$

Its order is the degree: $|\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)| = [E : F]$. An automorphism sends each root of a polynomial over $F$ to a root of the same polynomial, so the group permutes the roots.

## Examples

| Extension | Galois group |
|---|---|
| $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by complex conjugation |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the Klein four-group |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt{p_k})/\mathbb{Q}$ | $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^k$ |
| $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}/\mathbb{F}_q$ | $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the [[Frobenius endomorphism]] |
| splitting field of $x^5 - 4x + 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ | the symmetric group $S_5$ |

In the third row, $p_1, \ldots, p_k$ are distinct primes. In the last row, $S_5$ is not a solvable group, so the equation $x^5 - 4x + 2 = 0$ cannot be solved in radicals.

The [[Fundamental theorem of Galois theory]] matches the subgroups of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$ with the fields between $F$ and $E$.

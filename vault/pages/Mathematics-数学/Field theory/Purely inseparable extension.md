A **purely inseparable extension** $K/k$ of fields of [[Characteristic]] $p > 0$ is one in which every element of $K$ is a root of an equation

$$x^q = a, \qquad q = p^n, \quad a \in k.$$

It is the opposite of a [[Separable extension]]: an element of $K$ is separable over $k$ only if it already lies in $k$.

## Example

Over $k = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$, the polynomial $x^p - t$ is [[Irreducible polynomial|irreducible]], and in $K = k(t^{1/p})$ it becomes $\left(x - t^{1/p}\right)^p$. So $K/k$ has degree $p$, and its only automorphism is the identity. The [[Fundamental theorem of Galois theory|Galois correspondence]] breaks down here.

## Properties

- If $F \subseteq K \subseteq E$ and $E/F$ is purely inseparable, then so are $K/F$ and $E/K$. Conversely, if $K/F$ and $E/K$ are purely inseparable, so is $E/F$.
- The degree of a finite purely inseparable extension is a power of $p$.
- Every algebraic extension $E/F$ is a purely inseparable extension of a separable one: with $S$ the separable closure of $F$ in $E$, the extension $S/F$ is separable and $E/S$ is purely inseparable.

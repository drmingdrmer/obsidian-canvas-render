A [[Field]] $K$ is **perfect** if any one of these equivalent conditions holds:

- every [[Irreducible polynomial|irreducible]] polynomial over $K$ has no multiple roots in any extension;
- every irreducible polynomial over $K$ has a nonzero [[形式导数|formal derivative]];
- every irreducible polynomial over $K$ is [[Separable polynomial|separable]];
- every algebraic extension of $K$ is a [[Separable extension]];
- $K$ has characteristic $0$, or $K$ has characteristic $p$ and the [[Frobenius endomorphism]] $x \mapsto x^p$ is an automorphism of $K$.

Otherwise $K$ is **imperfect**.

## Examples

- Every field of characteristic zero, such as $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$.
- Every [[Finite field]] $\mathbb{F}_q$.
- Every algebraically closed field.

The field $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ is imperfect: $x^p - t$ is irreducible over it but inseparable, and its root $t^{1/p}$ generates a [[Purely inseparable extension]].

Over a perfect field, Galois theory is simpler: separability holds by itself, so every finite [[Normal extension]] is a [[Galois extension]].

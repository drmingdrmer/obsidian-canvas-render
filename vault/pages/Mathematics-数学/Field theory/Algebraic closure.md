An **algebraic closure** of a [[Field]] $K$ is an [[Algebraic extension]] of $K$ that is algebraically closed: every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root in it. Every field has an algebraic closure, unique up to an isomorphism that fixes $K$, so one speaks of *the* algebraic closure $\overline{K}$.

$\overline{K}$ is the largest algebraic extension of $K$, since every algebraic extension of $K$ embeds in it. It is also the smallest algebraically closed field that contains $K$.

## Examples

- By the fundamental theorem of algebra, $\overline{\mathbb{R}} = \mathbb{C}$.
- $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is the field of algebraic numbers.
- $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is countably infinite. It is the union of the [[Finite field|finite fields]] $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ for all $n$.

## Separable closure

Inside $\overline{K}$, the elements that are separable over $K$ form the **separable closure** $K^{\text{sep}}$, the largest [[Separable extension]] of $K$. It is all of $\overline{K}$ exactly when $K$ is a [[Perfect field]].

Every polynomial over $K$ splits in $\overline{K}$, so its [[Splitting field]] can be taken inside $\overline{K}$.

The **characteristic** of a [[Field]] $F$, written $\operatorname{char}(F)$, is the smallest positive $n$ such that

$$\underbrace{1 + \cdots + 1}_{n \text{ summands}} = 0,$$

or $0$ if there is no such $n$. The characteristic of a field is $0$ or a prime $p$: if it were a product $mn$ of smaller numbers, then $m \cdot 1$ and $n \cdot 1$ would be zero divisors.

## Characteristic zero

A field of characteristic zero contains a copy of $\mathbb{Q}$. Examples are $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, every algebraic number field, and every ordered field.

## Prime characteristic

A field of characteristic $p$ contains a copy of $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

- Every [[Finite field]] $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ has characteristic $p$.
- Some infinite fields do too, such as the field $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ of rational functions, and the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$.
- $p$ divides every binomial coefficient $\binom{p}{k}$ with $0 < k < p$, so $x \mapsto x^p$ respects addition: $(a + b)^p = a^p + b^p$. This map is the [[Frobenius endomorphism]].

Inseparable polynomials and extensions exist only in prime characteristic; see [[Separable polynomial]] and [[Perfect field]].
